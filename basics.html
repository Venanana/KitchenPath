Cooking basics

A few things to know before you start cooking.

  • Pantry staples

    Salt, pepper, olive oil, garlic, onions, rice, pasta, eggs, canned tomatoes, and soy sauce. That's the backbone of dozens of meals.

  • Kitchen tools

    A chef's knife, a cutting board, a non-stick pan, a saucepan, and a wooden spoon. Everything else is optional at the start.

  • Learn heat control

    Medium heat is your default — not high. Learn what "shimmering oil" and "gentle simmer" look like before you cook a real meal.

  • Cook one egg three ways

    Fried, scrambled, and boiled. It teaches timing, fat, and temperature for pennies per attempt.

Knife safety & basic cuts

A sharp knife is safer than a dull one.

Curl your fingertips into a "claw" so your knuckles guide the blade. Keep the tip of the knife on the board and rock it forward and back rather than chopping straight down.

  • Rough chop: uneven chunks — fine for soups and sauces.
  • Slice: even, flat pieces — good for onions and peppers.
  • Dice: small cubes — for anything you want to cook fast.
🔥

Heat & pan temperature

Most beginner food burns because the pan was too hot.

Preheat the pan for 2 minutes before adding oil. Test with a drop of water: it should sizzle and vanish, not sit there or explode. Add food in a single layer — crowding steams instead of browns.

  • Low: melting butter, scrambling eggs slowly, simmering sauces.
  • Medium: almost everything — onions, pancakes, chicken breast.
  • High: searing steak, boiling water, stir-frying.
🧂

Seasoning & tasting

Taste while you cook, not just at the end.

Add salt in small amounts throughout cooking rather than all at once at the end. If a dish tastes flat but not salty, it usually needs acid — a squeeze of lemon or a splash of vinegar.

  • Salt brings out flavor.
  • Acid brightens and balances richness.
  • Fat adds body and carries flavor.
🍚

Cooking grains & pasta

Salt the water like the sea.

Use far more water than you think you need, and salt it generously. For pasta, save a cup of the starchy cooking water before draining — it turns any sauce silky when you stir it back in.

  • Pasta: cook 1 minute less than the box says, finish in the sauce.
  • Rice: 1 part rice to 2 parts water, lid on, don't stir, rest 10 minutes.
🐔

Cooking protein safely

No thermometer? Use time and visual cues.

Chicken is done at 74°C / 165°F — the juices run clear and the meat is white all the way through with no pink. Eggs and fish are far more forgiving than beginners expect.

  • Pat meat dry before searing — wet meat steams, dry meat browns.
  • Let cooked meat rest 5 minutes so juices stay inside.
🧊

Storing & reheating food

Cool leftovers fast, then refrigerate.

Don't leave food at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Split big pots into shallow containers so they cool quickly, and refrigerate within 90 minutes.

  • Most cooked leftovers are good for 3–4 days.
  • Reheat until steaming hot all the way through.
